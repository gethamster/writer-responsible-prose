# Why this cadence exists

Two sources stacked, neither of them bad English, both of them wrong for American written register.

## 1. Hindi / Indo-Aryan information structure

Hindi and most Indo-Aryan languages mark given vs new information by **position**, not by articles or relative clauses.

- Topic (already known) comes first.
- Comment (new) comes after.
- Recoverable nouns **drop**. If "spare capacity" is in the air, the speaker says *spare*.
- Focus particles like Hindi *hī* become Indian English *only* / *itself* (`I am here only`, `tomorrow itself`).
- Definiteness is often inferred from clause position, so articles wobble and bare nouns survive in writing.

Indian English imported the spoken machinery into prose (Lange 2012 on ICE-India: topicalization, left dislocation, unlinked topics, clefts). Written office English added a colonial bureaucratic overlay of passives, nominalizations, *kindly*, *do the needful*, *revert*, and *the same*.

Contrastive rhetoric names the result **reader-responsible** prose, where the writer assumes you already hold the topic. American published prose is **writer-responsible**: the writer names the noun and hangs the label off it.

These feel unfinished to an American reader because the noun or the hinge is missing:

- `Spare moves around the day.` — topic hung, noun dropped.
- `X. That is incremental revenue.` — comment delivered as a caption after a full stop.
- `A market is what lets quiet hours clear.` — essence named with a cleft instead of a relative.

Don't put the topic down, stop, and then say what it is. Write the topic and the comment in one sentence.

## 2. LLM explanatory reward

Instruction-tuned models independently learned a cousin of the same shape.

- Short equative sentences score as "clear."
- `That is Y` after a period scores as "helpful restatement."
- `X is what Y` scores as "insight."
- Abstract nominal subjects (`the business case`, `utilization`) score as "professional."

You get Indian-office information structure wearing LLM punch: topic, caption, essence cleft, next abstraction.

## What American writing does instead

It **subordinates**.

- Fact, label.
- Fact, which consequence.
- Fact so consequence.

It repeats nouns, uses *which* / *so* / *because* instead of *is what*, and lets a sentence do two jobs instead of stopping to name what it just said.

If a draft keeps going `Fact. Relabel. Essence. Abstract subject.` the substrate is still running, so fold until it stops.
